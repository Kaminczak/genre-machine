# Cinderella - Graphic Novel

**Logline:** Ash-grey panels give way to one full-color spread at the transformation, then back to grey at the stroke of twelve.

## Script

("PAGE ONE: a wide gray panel, a kitchen, a girl small in the corner of it, and the panel itself is drawn smudged — the artist inks this whole world in ash-gray wash, and her alone in clean line. CAPTION: they named her for what they left her. PAGES TWO AND THREE: the tellings-in-miniature — stepsisters in curl papers, an invitation with gilt edges rendered in the book's first metallic ink, a door closing. Grays. Then the hearth panel: her tears fall into the ash, and where each lands, the wash blooms white. PAGE FOUR IS THE ONE THE MEDIUM WAS MADE FOR. The godmother does not appear in a panel. The godmother IS the page: the gutters between panels curl and gather and become a figure whose body is the white space itself, holding every scene at once, and her dialogue is not in balloons but written along the panel borders, because she speaks from outside the story's frames: little one, the borders read, we are going to redraw you. THE TRANSFORMATION SPREAD: one full-bleed image, the book's single explosion of color — the gown igniting panel by panel up her body, ash-gray to ember-orange to a gold the printer had to be talked into, and on her feet, two panels all their own: slippers drawn in no color at all, pure white paper, glass rendered as absence. The ball is three pages of color slowly winning: the prince's panels shed their gray as he approaches her. The dance is a double-page spiral of panels with no gutters, time gone liquid. THEN: a clock face, full page, and the artist's cruelest choice — midnight is rendered as the return of the gutters. The white spaces harden back into borders. The color drains panel by panel in her flight down the stairs, gold to ember to ash, and the final panel of the sequence is just her foot, bare, mid-step, and behind it the slipper on the stair: white paper, clean line, the last unspoiled shape in a gray world. FINAL PAGE, nine panels: the prince, in slow color, lifting it. The stepsisters' feet, gray, gray. The kitchen door. Her face. The fit — drawn without a sound effect, the book's only silent panel with a border of gold. LAST PANEL, full width: the two of them small in a doorway, and the whole panel — for the first time, edge to edge, gutters and all — printed in color. CAPTION: some stories end when the ash runs out.",)
