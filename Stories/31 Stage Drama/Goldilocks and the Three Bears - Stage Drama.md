# Goldilocks and the Three Bears - Stage Drama

**Logline:** A single set: three chairs, three bowls, three beds. Goldilocks enters. The Bears wait in the wings for the whole first act.

## Script

("A single set: the Ursine kitchen, hearth downstage right, three chairs in ascending size, a staircase suggesting rooms above. On the table, three bowls, practical, steaming — the stage manager refills them nightly; the steam is the play's first line. Enter GOLDILOCKS, though the program calls her THE GIRL, in a coat too thin for the painted weather. She speaks to the audience while she does it — that is the conceit, she narrates her own trespass, giving us her reasons in the second person: you would have gone in too, you have gone in, everyone has a house they entered that was not theirs. She eats. We watch. It takes as long as eating takes; the director insists. She breaks the chair and the crack is the loudest sound of the act, and she holds the pieces up to us like evidence for the defense. She climbs the stairs. The stage darkens, except the hearth. And then the bears — and here is the trick of it — the bears enter through the house, up the center aisle, past us, around us, three actors in beautiful terrible masks moving through the audience we have been sitting in all along, and we understand, too late, whose house we are in, and whose bowls, and who has been watching whom. The smallest bear stops at the stairs. He looks back. At us. CURTAIN.",)
