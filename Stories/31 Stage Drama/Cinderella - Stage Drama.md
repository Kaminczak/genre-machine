# Cinderella - Stage Drama

**Logline:** Two acts. Act One: the hearth. Act Two: the ball. Between them, a costume change performed in full view of the audience.

## Script

("Two acts, one interval, no curtain between the worlds. ACT ONE is the hearth: a kitchen built in loving, oppressive detail — working pump, real ash, the audience close enough to smell the cold grease. The girl scours pots in real time; the director is adamant; scouring takes what scouring takes. Her stepsisters dress for the ball ONSTAGE, at mirrors angled so the audience is the mirror — they adjust themselves at us, they ask us is this becoming, and we, complicit, say nothing. The invitation is read aloud twice, neither time to her. When the house empties, she speaks her one monologue to the fire, and the fire — a trick of silk and fans and light — rises, and takes the shape of a woman. THE TRANSFORMATION IS THE INTERVAL. This is the play's scandal and its genius: there is no blackout. The change happens in full view, during the interval, while the house lights are half up and the audience is deciding whether to go for a drink. Stagehands in godmother-gray walk on openly. They dress her, piece by piece, gown over kitchen dress, glass slippers over bare feet, while the fire-woman supervises and the audience — every night, the stage manager logs it — falls silent and stays. Nobody goes for the drink. We watch a woman being made ready by many hands, which is, the program notes observe, what a miracle actually looks like. ACT TWO is the ball, and the ballroom is the kitchen — same set, transfigured by light, the pump now a fountain, the hearth now a hearth. The prince dances with her among guests who are the stepsisters' mirrors repurposed, so the couple multiplies to infinity. The clock does not strike; the ash does. It begins to fall from the flies at the first stroke, gray and silent, and she runs against a snowfall of ash as the ballroom light dies back into kitchen light around her — the two worlds shown to be one world, which was the thesis all along. One slipper remains downstage center. The prince picks it up in kitchen-light, among pots. He looks at the hearth. He looks at us. BLACKOUT, at last, and in it, the sound the whole play has withheld: a knock at a door.",)
