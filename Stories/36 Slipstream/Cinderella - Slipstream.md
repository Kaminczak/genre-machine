# Cinderella - Slipstream

**Logline:** The ball happens every night and has never happened. Cinderella keeps finding one shoe already gone at sunset. The prince remembers her from tomorrow.

## Script

("The ball is every night and has never happened; this is generally understood and never said, like most of what the household runs on. Each evening at six the stepsisters dress for it, and each midnight they return from it, and no one can name the road it sits on; the coachman, asked directly, gave notice. Ella's chores exist in the same weather: the hearth she sweeps is sometimes swept already, by her, later — she has learned to leave notes for herself across the loop, and the notes are sometimes answered in her hand before she writes them. The godmother arrives on the nights Ella cries, which is how Ella learned to cry strategically, which the godmother says is the correct use of grief in a house like this. The transformation varies. The pumpkin has been a carriage, a sedan, a boat in a year with no water; once, memorably, it stayed a pumpkin and the mice carried it anyway, out of loyalty. The gown is always her mother's, though her mother never owned it; ownership works differently for the dead, the godmother explains, they own what should have been theirs. Only the slippers are constant. Glass, size six, cold at sunset and warm by the last dance, and they are the one object that persists across every version of the night — she has found them on her feet at breakfast, in the icebox, once on the stepmother's shelf being called a vase. The prince is constant too, in his way: every night he knows her a little sooner. The first year, the last dance. This year, the door. He remembers forward, he apologized once, at the wrong end of an evening. It is rude. I will know you before we meet soon, and then what will we do. At midnight she runs, because the running is load-bearing; the versions where she stayed are the ones the house does not speak of, the ones the stepsisters came home early from, pale. She loses the left slipper on the stair. It is on her foot at breakfast. The fit, when it finally happens — when he arrives some morning at the kitchen door holding the shoe she is already wearing — will end the loop, or begin it; the godmother says these are the same door from different sides. Tuesday, the notes in her own hand have started saying. Nothing else. Just: Tuesday. She has begun, quietly, packing.",)
