# Little Red Riding Hood - Slipstream

**Logline:** Red walks a path that is sometimes a hallway. Grandmother keeps being someone else's grandmother. The teeth are consistent.

## Script

The path to grandmother's is twenty minutes, except on the days it is a hallway. Red has stopped mentioning this. On hallway days the trees hold doorframes, and the doors are all ajar, and behind them grandmothers — dozens, wrong ones, each in a correct bed. She checks them like hotel rooms. Too small. Too kind. This one is somebody's grandmother, clearly loved, but not hers; the cakes do not know her. The wolf is not always there, but the teeth always are: on the nightstand, in a glass; strung on a cord above a crib; once, politely, in the butter dish. What big teeth. The phrase waits in her mouth every visit like a key she has not found the lock for. On Thursday the path was a path, and grandmother was her own grandmother, propped on pillows, entirely accounted for, and that was somehow the unbearable day — the day Red stood in the doorway counting details, and could not stop, and grandmother watched her count and said, not unkindly: I know, dear. I checked you twice myself. Sit down. The tea only knows us on the days we agree to match.
