**Logline:** A blonde traveler accepts hospitality at a forest hearth where three bear-folk live. Three bowls of bramble-stew, three woven chairs, three nests.

## Script

("The traveler came down the ridge path at midday, golden hair full of road dust, and the bear-folk's door stood open because in Greenhollow a closed door means only that you are out. The custom is written on the lintel: eat what cools, mend what breaks, rest where there is room. Three bowls of bramble-stew sat cooling on the sill — the big one still volcanic, the middle chilled by the springhouse shade, the small one just right — and she ate the small one to the glaze, as the lintel said she might. The small woven chair gave under her road-heavy bones, and she set the splinters by the door with an apology note tucked in, as the custom asks. Then she slept in the smallest nest, deep and honest. The bear-folk came home from the terraces and read the house like a letter: stew eaten, chair folded into kindling with a note, a traveler asleep. They woke her gently for supper. She stayed three days, rewove the chair herself with willow from the creek — better than before, the small one admitted — and left seeds from her own country in the dooryard. That is the whole story.
