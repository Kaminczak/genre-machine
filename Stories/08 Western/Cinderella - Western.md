# Cinderella - Western

**Logline:** A homestead daughter is gifted a saloon-night dress by a card-sharp godmother. A glass-heeled boot stays at the territorial governor's ball.

## Script

("The Territory Governor's ball came once a year to Dry Creek, and the Hollis widow meant her two daughters to leave it engaged or ruined trying. Her stepdaughter Ella she meant to leave home, in the cookhouse, where the girl had lived since her daddy's claim and her daddy's heart gave out the same August. What the widow did not figure on was Reyes — the card-sharp who wintered over the livery, who owed Ella's father a debt from the old days that she had decided, at last, to pay. She paid it in style: a gown won off a San Francisco widow, a buggy conjured from the Meachum pumpkin wagon and two coats of lacquer, and boots — glass-heeled, Spanish make, absurd and beautiful, take them, kid, I won them off a woman who danced in them across two territories. Be back by midnight, Reyes said, that is when the livery man sobers and counts his buggies. The Governor's son danced every dance with the girl nobody in the Territory could place. At midnight she lit out at a dead run and left one glass heel on the Governor's porch, and the boy — to the Territory's everlasting entertainment — rode ranch to ranch with it in a flour sack, trying it on every foot from Dry Creek to the county line. It fit the cookhouse girl at the Hollis place. The widow said there must be some mistake. There is, said the Governor's son, and it has been living in your cookhouse.",)
