# Little Red Riding Hood - Biography Memoir

**Logline:** An old woman in a faded red coat writes the account of the day she walked into the woods, what met her there, and the sixty years she spent not talking about it.

## Script

I am eighty-one years old, and I have worn some red every day of my life, and I have never once told the whole of it. My granddaughter asks for the story the way you ask for a song, and I give her the song: the path, the flowers, the wolf, the woodsman. The song is true. Songs usually are. What I never say: that I saw him follow me from the village and said nothing, because a girl who raises alarms about respectable-looking men learned quickly, in that country, which of them the village believed. That grandmother fought — the overturned lamp, the wardrobe door split at the hinge, details the song files under miracle. That the woodsman came because grandmother had paid him for years to walk past at four o'clock, because she had met wolves before, because the women of my family have always kept an appointment we do not explain. I keep it still. Every afternoon at four, a neighbor walks past my gate, and waves, and I wave. My granddaughter thinks it is charming. One day I will tell her what it is. It is the song, still being sung.
