# Little Red Riding Hood - Climate Fiction

**Logline:** The woods burned twice and the path is ash. Red carries water to grandmother's cabin past a wolf starved down out of the hills.

## Script

The woods burned twice — the second time in the year nobody could pretend anymore — and what is left is called the woods out of courtesy: black poles, ash meadow, the green stubble of the replanting rows. Red walks the firebreak road with two canteens and grandmother's insulin, cold-packed, because grandmother will not leave the cabin. Someone has to stay, she says on the radio, or it stops being a place and becomes an acreage. The wolf came down from the hills in August, starved out, ribs like a shipwreck. It does not stalk her, exactly. It walks the firebreak too, at a distance that has become a kind of agreement, drinking where she spills water for it in the burned culvert, because her grandmother told her to. It ate from the hills your whole life, grandmother says. The hills are gone. That makes it family. At the cabin they sit on the porch, three of them now, at three distances, and watch the replanting rows for the particular green that means the roots have taken. Sixty years, grandmother says. I will not see it. Water the wolf.
