# Cinderella - Climate Fiction

**Logline:** The kingdom's last glassmaker fires slippers from storm-melted sand. Cinderella rides to the ball through the flood zone, and midnight is a curfew, not a curse.

## Script

("The estate had a seawall, and the seawall had a date, and everyone on the coast knew both even if the Comtesse — that is what the stepmother styled herself, inland money playing at coastal gentry — refused to read the surveys. Ella read them. Ella was the one who sandbagged the kitchen doors each surge season, hauled the generator to the loft, kept the salt out of the cistern: the estate's one competent pair of hands, inherited with the property like a fixture. The Governor's Climate Ball — that was truly its name; the coast had reached the stage of naming its galas after the emergency — was invitation-only, and hers went to a stepsister, naturally. The glassmaker came to the kitchen door at dusk. The last one on the coast: an old woman who had stayed when the workshops moved to high ground, who fired her furnace with driftwood from the wrecks and made — this was her whole late-life catalog — objects from storm-melted sand. Sand the sea had already claimed once, fused by her fire into things that could not be claimed again. I knew your mother, she said, which on that coast was a whole genealogy. The gown was sail-silk from a decommissioned rescue sloop. The slippers were her masterwork: storm-glass, sea-colored, with the old wreck's iron in them for strength — shoes made, she said, from everything the water thought it had taken. And midnight is not a spell, child; midnight is the tide. The causeway floods at twelve. You will want to be on this side of it. The Governor's son had spent the ball being lobbied by inland money; he danced instead with the one guest who could read the harbor charts on the wall. She left at 11:40 — she had built margin into midnight, she was not new to water — and the slipper she lost at the causeway stair was under six inches of tide by twelve and in his hand by morning, sea-colored, full of sea. He did not try it on the county's feet. He took it to the last glassmaker on the coast, who looked at her own work a long time and said: the girl this fits has been keeping your father's coast alive for free. Go to the kitchen door, and bring the surveys. He brought the surveys. The wedding was on the seawall. The wall's date, under the new administration's works program, has been moved back forty years, and the causeway, on the coast's new maps, is named for her.",)
