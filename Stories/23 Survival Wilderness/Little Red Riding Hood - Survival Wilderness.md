# Little Red Riding Hood - Survival Wilderness

**Logline:** A girl in a red parka treks the alpine pass to grandmother's remote cabin. A timber wolf pack tracks her; the chimney has been cold for hours.

## Script

The pass to grandmother's cabin is four hours in good weather, and the weather had stopped being good at dawn. Red went anyway; the radio call had cut off mid-sentence, and grandmother did not waste sentences. Red wore the red parka — visibility red, her grandmother's rule: if the mountain takes you, make it show its work. Snow to the knee by the second ridge. The pack found her at the treeline, five shapes flowing gray between the trunks, patient as arithmetic. She did not run; you cannot outrun arithmetic. She walked, downwind of nothing, upwind of everything, her ice axe loose in her hand, and talked to them in a low steady voice about absolutely nothing, the way grandmother taught. The cabin chimney showed no smoke, which put a colder thing in her than the wind. She found grandmother on the floor with a splinted ankle and a dead radio battery, furious at the inconvenience. Red lit the stove. The pack circled twice, read the smoke, and went to find easier arithmetic. They ate the cakes at midnight, rationed, laughing.
