# Cinderella - Survival Wilderness

**Logline:** An exiled girl trapped in mountain servitude is granted a single warm night by a hermit godmother. A glass climbing-cleat is left at the prince's chalet.

## Script

("The high station was six weeks from anywhere when the first snows shut the pass, and the girl they wintered as unpaid help — her stepmother ran the way-house, and ran her — knew the mountain better than any of them, because the mountain was where she was sent for everything the house ran short of. Wood. Water. Silence. The Governor's midwinter ball, down-valley, was a two-day trek in good weather; her stepfamily took the sledge and the good furs and left her the goats. The hermit came at dusk. Every range has one — the old woman up past the treeline that the way-house guests told stories about, whom the girl had been leaving food for, on her own ration, for three winters, because you do not let anyone winter alone above the trees. The hermit had come to settle accounts. She dressed the girl in what she had carried up the mountain a lifetime ago and never worn again: a gown from a capital that no longer existed, and glass-heeled boots with crampon points, made, she said, for dancing on ice, in a country that danced on ice, by a man who thought I would stay. The way down was hers alone: the ridge route, the frozen falls, the shortcuts that only someone who had hauled this mountain's water could walk in the dark. She reached the ball at ten, danced on glass crampons with the Governor's son till midnight, and left when the wind turned, because she could read the wind and nobody else there could. She took the storm route home. He found the boot she lost at the falls in the spring melt, point-down in the ice like a flag, and did the only sensible thing a valley boy could do: he hired a guide, learned the mountain, and came up the hard way, asking at every station for the girl who could outwalk weather. The way-house claimed no such girl. The goats, the story goes, gave her up — following her down from the spring pasture right through the middle of his questions. They wintered together at the high station every year after. The hermit came to the wedding. She wore the other boot.",)
