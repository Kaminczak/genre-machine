# Cinderella - Transgressive

**Logline:** Cinderella scrubs floors by day and burns her stepmother's ledgers by night. The prince is an exit, not a love story, and she takes it with her eyes open.

## Script

("Everyone loves the pumpkin. Nobody asks about the ledger. Here is the ledger: my father died owing, my stepmother inherited the owing and me in the same probate, and the arrangement we do not call servitude was me working the debt at a wage set by the creditor, who was also the landlord, who was also legally my mother. The ball was not a dream. The ball was a labor market — the season's daughters priced and paraded, and my stepsisters entered like invoices, and I was kept home as depreciated stock. So understand what the godmother actually was. She was not kindness from nowhere; kindness from nowhere had never once appeared in that house, I had run the experiment for years. She was my mother's sister, estranged the way inconvenient women are estranged, and she arrived not with a wand but with paperwork — the original deed, the disputed will, and a dress. The dress was strategy. The glass slippers were strategy: wear the one thing so absurd, so memorable, so uninsurable, that the room cannot file you under staff. Go be seen by power, she said, and leave before it can appraise you; scarcity is the only pricing signal these people respect. It worked. Of course it worked; it was designed by a woman who had watched the machine eat her sister. I danced with the prince, and the honest version is: he was the softest man I had ever met, softness being what that much cushioning buys, and I liked him, and I resented liking him, and at midnight I did the strategic thing and the true thing at once — I ran, and I left the slipper deliberately. The stories say lost. I placed it. On the stair, center, where he could not miss it: a glass shoe, an impossible object, a debt the palace now owed me. Let power come to the kitchen door for once. Let power try things on. When he came — and he came, holding my collateral like it might hatch — he found the ledger too, because I put it in his other hand. Marry me if you like, I said, but audit them first. He did both, in that order. The wedding was small. The judgment was enormous. They call it a fairy tale because the alternative is calling it a repossession, and the pumpkin, for the record, we ate.",)
