# Goldilocks and the Three Bears - 1950s Suburban Mystery

**Logline:** A blonde new neighbor lets herself into the Ursines' ranch home. Casseroles still warm. Recliners still indented. The bedrooms — too perfect.

## Script

("The Ursines of 44 Maplecrest were the kind of family the neighborhood approved of: casseroles to every potluck, lawn like a putting green, three of everything and all of it polished. So when the new girl from the rental on Sycamore let herself in that Tuesday — the door was open, she would say later, and in a way it was — she found the kitchen still warm. Three casseroles cooling on trivets: one bubbling hot, one refrigerator-cold in a way that made no sense at 2 P.M., one exactly, welcomingly warm. She ate a corner of it, then a middle of it. She sat in the den: Father's recliner, rigid; Mother's chair, soft as quicksand; the boy's — but the boy's chair looked unsat-in. The whole house, she realized, looked staged, like a model home wearing a family as a costume. Upstairs, the beds: made, made, made, hospital corners, and the smallest one's corners were wrong — remade in a hurry, by different hands. She was asleep in it when the Ursines came home at five, all smiles, and stood in the bedroom doorway in a row. We are so glad you stopped by, said Mrs. Ursine, pleasantly. We have been hoping someone would. Nobody on Maplecrest has seen the girl from Sycamore since, but the casserole at the next potluck was, everyone agreed, just right.",)
