# Cinderella - Supernatural Horror

**Logline:** A girl who sleeps in the ashes is visited by something that says it is her mother. The gown is real. The carriage is real. The price is not disclosed until midnight.

## Script

("The thing about the gift was that no one made her take it. That is what the house wants understood; that is why there is a telling at all. The girl slept by the hearth of her dead mother's house while her stepmother let the rooms above go to dust and spite, and on the night of the ball she wept into the banked coals, and something in the chimney wept back. It said it was her mother. It knew the lullaby. It knew where the loose brick was, and what was behind it: a gown, folded wrong — folded the way you fold a thing with a body still in mind — and slippers of glass, cold as marrow, that fit as if they had been waiting inside her all along. Wear me down, the voice said, and be seen, and come home by midnight, and it did not say or, and that was the mercy, or the trap; the tellings differ. She was the beauty of the ball. Everyone saw her; no one remembered her face after, only the cold coming off the glass, only that dancing near her felt like a window left open in January. The prince alone was not chilled. The prince felt warm for the first time in — and here his household went quiet in the retellings — years. At the twelfth stroke she ran, because the voice in the chimney had begun speaking out of the slippers themselves, closer than any chimney, closer than skin: stay, it said now. Stay past the hour and we need never go home at all. She tore the left one off on the stair and ran on a bare foot and a shod one, and the shod foot, the villagers say, has been cold ever since, cold in July, cold in wool. The prince keeps the left slipper in a case of rowan wood, because his grandmother knew things. On winter nights it fogs from the inside. Something breathes in there, his household says. Something waits to be worn down. The girl married no prince. The girl became the grandmother who knows things, and her rule is the one the valley keeps to this day: take nothing from a voice that knows your lullaby until you have asked it to sing the second verse. The dead know only the verses we sang them. The other things in the chimney know only the first.",)
