# Cinderella - Courtroom Drama

**Logline:** Cinderella v. Estate of her late father: a will contested, a stepmother's claim, the prince as character witness. A glass slipper, Exhibit A.

## Script

("In re the Estate of Aldous Endry, and the consolidated matter of Endry v. Endry. The court has heard four days of testimony concerning a household, a ball, and an article of footwear, and makes the following findings. One: the decedent's will left the house and its contents to his daughter, with a life tenancy for his second wife. The will was mislaid for six years, during which the daughter's tenancy in her own house was, in the widow's phrase, in a domestic capacity, and in the court's phrase, unpaid labor extracted under color of grief. Two: the events of the Royal Charity Ball are not materially disputed. The daughter attended in her late mother's gown and slippers, retrieved from a sealed attic her stepmother testified was empty — the second empty container of this proceeding, the court notes, a habit of emptiness the court finds probative throughout. Three: the slipper deposited on the palace stair at midnight and entered here as Exhibit A is glass, size six, and matches both its recovered mate, Exhibit B, produced by the daughter, and the pair depicted in the late Mrs. Endry's portrait, Exhibit C. The widow's claim that the slippers were her own was tested by the court's own experiment, over objection, in open session. The court will not describe the attempt at length. The record may reflect that it failed at the instep. Judgment: the will is admitted; the house passes to the daughter; the tenancy is revoked for waste, the waste being six years of a young woman's life. The Crown's separate inquiry — the young man of the palace and his purposes with Exhibit A — is not this court's jurisdiction. The court merely observes that Exhibit A has fit exactly one person in this county, that she has her house back, and that the young man in question has been waiting in the gallery all four days. Adjourned.",)
