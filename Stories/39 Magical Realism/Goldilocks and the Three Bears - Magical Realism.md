# Goldilocks and the Three Bears - Magical Realism

**Logline:** The bears' house sets three places every morning by itself, and has for years. The village says a golden-haired girl lives in the walls. She does, and it's fine.

## Script

("The house in the birch clearing set its own table, and had for as long as the village kept memory: three bowls filled each dawn from no pot anyone tended, three chairs squared to the table, three beds made with corners like salutes. The Osín family had lived there once — bearish, big-shouldered folk, gone two generations now — and the house had simply declined to notice their leaving, the way a widow declines to set one plate fewer. The village worked around it, as you do: the miller's road bent to miss the clearing, and children were told the house is still keeping house, be polite. The girl in the walls arrived one October — golden-haired, from the war in the next valley over, nobody asked which war, there is always a war in the next valley over — and the house, everyone agreed, grew happier at once. The dawn bowls steamed harder. A fourth chair appeared on the porch, child-sized, and no one had built it. You could see her some evenings at the upstairs window, brushing her hair, and the correct thing was to wave, and she would wave, and that was that. The priest raised the matter once, delicately, of whose house it lawfully was. The village considered. It is the house's house, said the miller's wife finally, and the girl is who the house was setting the table for. Mass moved on. The house, at Christmas, put lights in its own windows, and the village brought her cake, and it was, they still say it every year, just right.",)
