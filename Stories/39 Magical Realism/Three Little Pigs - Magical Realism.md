# Three Little Pigs - Magical Realism

**Logline:** The brick house has stood for four hundred years, grieving. The wolf's wind is treated as a season, and nobody in the village remarks on it.

## Script

In the village the wind arrived every year on the feast of Saint Anselm, and nobody called it a wolf, though it had teeth and a name and, some years, an opinion about doors. The first brother built of straw because straw remembers being wheat, and he was sentimental. His house sighed away one evening between the soup and the bread, and he carried his chair to the second brother's, whose stick house had taken root in the spring and now fruited small green plums no one dared eat. The wind took that one more slowly, out of respect for the plums. The third house was brick and had been brick for four hundred years, since the great-great-grandmother laid it with mortar mixed from ash and patience, and it grieved on Sundays, audibly, which the neighbors considered good luck. When the wind circled it — courting it, furious — the house only grieved louder, and the wind grew embarrassed, as the young do around old sorrow. Inside, the pot on the hearth had never once been allowed to cool. The day it cools, the grandmothers say, the wind will finally get a name.
