# Little Red Riding Hood - Historical Fiction

**Logline:** A girl in a red kerchief carries bread past checkpoints to her grandmother in a besieged city. The officer who waves her through has very good teeth.

## Script

The city had been under siege for four months, and bread had become a language. Red crossed it twice a week: a girl with a basket and a red kerchief, waved through checkpoints by soldiers who had decided she was harmless, which was the point of the kerchief, and of the girl. Grandmother lived in the old quarter, fourth floor, and could no longer manage the stairs, and what the basket carried under the bread was other bread: leaflets, the thin kind, the kind you die for. The officer at the Karlova checkpoint was new that October. Polite. He had very good teeth, and he used them, smiling, learning her name, her route, her grandmother's street. On the fourth Friday he was waiting on the landing instead, in grandmother's doorway, holding a leaflet. Where is she, said Red. Gone, he said. Since Tuesday. I have been reading. He looked at the leaflet a long moment, and then folded it into his coat, and walked past her down the stairs, and did not report it. Some wolves, grandmother said later, in the safe cellar across the river, are only dogs the war put teeth on. Some. Do not test the ratio.
