# Goldilocks and the Three Bears - Historical Fiction

**Logline:** A blonde evacuee billeted in the countryside lets herself into the Ursine farmhouse. Rationing is on. The porridge is a crime worth hanging for.

## Script

("October 1943, and the evacuee arrived in the dales with a cardboard case and a name tag, billeted on the Ursell farm — a big quiet man, his wife, and their boy, all of them rationed to the last ounce like everyone. The girl was a city child, gold-haired, hungry in the way of that whole year. Her first afternoon, alone in the farmhouse while the family worked the top field, she found the larder unlocked: three bowls of porridge set to cool for supper, made on the last of the week's oats. She ate the small one. All of it. You who have never been hungry in 1943 may judge her; nobody in the dales that year did. What the boy said when the family found out has not survived; what the wife said has, because she said it every October after, at harvest supper, with the girl — by then grown, by then family — at the table: hunger came to our house wearing a name tag, and we thanked God it found the porridge and not the pride to starve quietly. They split three bowls four ways that winter, and the winters after. The bowl itself, blue-ringed, chipped, is in the county museum now, on a card that reads: LENT BY THE URSELL FAMILY. NEVER STOLEN. EATEN BY SOMEONE WE LOVED BEFORE WE KNEW HER.",)
