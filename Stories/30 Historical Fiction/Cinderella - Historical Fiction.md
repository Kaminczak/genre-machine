# Cinderella - Historical Fiction

**Logline:** A kitchen maid in a great house on the eve of revolution is dressed for one night at the governor's ball. She leaves a glass shoe on the stairs and the house burns by morning.

## Script

("In the last autumn of the old regime, the Comtesse d'Aubrec kept a great house on the river and, in its kitchens, her late husband's daughter, whom the household knew as Cendrine and the ration books did not know at all. The Governor's Ball that October was the ancien monde dancing on its own grave; everyone said so afterward, and some said so at the time. That Cendrine attended is fact: the gate ledger records an unnamed young woman, arrived on foot, admitted on the word of the old seamstress Mme. Perrault — herself a fixture of the servants' wing, herself due a chapter no history has given her. The gown is fact: silk, twenty years out of fashion, identified later as belonging to the first Mme. d'Aubrec, whose wardrobe the Comtesse believed burned. The slippers are the famous fact: glass, exposition ware from the ateliers, the frivolity of a world about to learn the price of frivolity. She danced, the accounts agree, with the Governor's son — the same Lieutenant d'Erville who would surface two years later on the other side of everything, a quartermaster for the new order. And at midnight she vanished, leaving one slipper on the river stair, and by morning it did not matter, because by morning the warehouses were burning and the old world had begun its short walk to the square. Here the story should end; history has no time for shoes. But the archives of the Transition Committee, opened to scholars only this generation, contain an anomaly: a requisition, in d'Erville's hand, for the contents of the d'Aubrec house, all chattels to the people — save one item, released to a citizen whose name is inked out, the release annotated in the margin: property of her mother. Restitution, not requisition. The citizen's receipt survives. It is signed only with an initial, C., and beneath it, in the same hand, a line the archivists at first took for a code and now, in the fuller light of everything, read plainly: one slipper, reunited with its pair. The pair outlived the house, the regime, and everyone named above. They are in the Museum of the Transition today, labeled ARTIFACT: ANCIEN MONDE. The card does not say what the archive says: that grace, like glass, survives the fire in fragments, and someone always comes back for it.",)
